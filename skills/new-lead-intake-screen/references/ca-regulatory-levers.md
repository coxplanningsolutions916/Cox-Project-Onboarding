# California Regulatory Levers

A screening reference for the state reform-law pathways that can unlock development potential beyond base zoning. Used in Stage 5.4 to flag candidates for the findings email and the proposed ISR scope.

> **Scope of use.** This file supports the **initial strategic screen**, not the paid analysis. In the free screen, name the plausible pathway, cite the governing code, and note the obvious disqualifiers — nothing more. The defensible determination (eligibility, exact yield, interaction with local ordinances and Housing Element status) is a Step 1 ISR deliverable. Never present a lever as secured.

> **Maintenance.** California amends housing law nearly every legislative cycle; section numbers shift on recodification (the ADU statutes were recodified in 2025). **Verify current statute text before relying on any entry in paid work** — entries marked "verify" rest on secondary sources or conflicting reports. This file is the single source of truth shared by the intake screen and ISR-stage regulatory analysis — update it once, in one place. Candidate canonical home: Cox Operations Manual (regulatory reference) or Component Library. Backfill lessons from each ISR (most recently A-Plus Construction) here. Last full review: 2026-10-11 (see Changelog).

## How to use in the screen

1. Start from the zoning and General Plan designations (Stage 5.1).
2. Match the prospect's intent (residential yield, conversion, subdivision, redevelopment) to the pathways below.
3. Flag the one or two with the strongest fit. For residential infill, prefer the simplest ministerial path that delivers the yield — ADU pathways routinely outperform density bonus on unit count and regulatory simplicity (ministerial, no CEQA, no affordability covenant, no prevailing wage).
4. For a discretionary infill project, also check the CEQA infill exemption below — it can matter as much as the housing pathway.

## Levers

### ADU / JADU (ministerial)
*Updated 2026-10.*
- **Unlocks:** Additional ministerial units on residential lots — primary + detached ADU + JADU stacking; no CEQA, no affordability covenant, no prevailing wage. On existing multifamily: conversions of non-living space plus up to 8 detached ADUs (not more than the number of existing units; SB 1211, 2024). Two detached ADUs with a proposed multifamily building.
- **Citation:** Gov. Code §§66310–66342 (recodified 2025; formerly §65852.2 / §65852.22). Exempt stacking combinations at §66323; 60-day approval clock at §66317.
- **Recent changes:**
  - *In effect 1 Jan 2026 (2025 bills):* SB 543 — completeness decision within 15 business days; size limits measured by interior livable space; school-fee exemption for ADUs ≤500 sq ft. AB 1154 — JADU owner-occupancy may be required only where the JADU shares a bathroom with the main house; JADU rentals >30 days. A separate 2025 SB 9 voids a local ADU ordinance not submitted to HCD or not corrected after HCD findings.
  - *Effective 1 Jan 2027 (2026 bills):* AB 956 (Ch. 791/2026) — two detached new-construction ADUs on a single-family lot (up from one). SB 1117 — impact fees on the first two ADUs only on livable space over 750 sq ft.
- **Disqualifiers / notes:** Size, height, and setback limits apply (agency may not cap below 850 sq ft / 1,000 sq ft with >1 bedroom, or require side/rear setbacks over 4 ft); HOA/CC&R and some overlay constraints interact. Pre-2020 unpermitted ADUs have a legalization path (AB 2533, 2024). Strongest lever for single-lot and small-infill residential.

### SB 9 — two-unit and urban lot split (ministerial)
*Updated 2026-10.*
- **Unlocks:** Up to two primary units per lot (local standards may not prevent two units of ≥800 sq ft each) plus a ministerial urban lot split into two lots of roughly equal size, each ≥1,200 sq ft, with up to two units on each (potential 4 units across the split). 60-day decision on a complete application.
- **Citation:** Gov. Code §65852.21 (two-unit) and §66411.7 (lot split). Amended by SB 450 (2024; tightened what local standards can apply) and AB 1061 (2025; historic exclusion narrowed to contributing structures and listed resources rather than whole districts).
- **Disqualifiers / notes:** Single-family-zoned lots in an urbanized area or urban cluster only. Owner-occupancy affidavit (3 years) applies to **lot splits only** — the two-unit path carries no owner-occupancy rule. Excluded: demolition/alteration of rent-restricted, rent-controlled, or tenant-occupied (last 3 years) housing; Ellis Act withdrawal in last 15 years; wetlands, prime farmland, mapped habitat, most very high fire hazard areas; covered historic resources. A split lot cannot be split again. Whether ADUs/JADUs must also be allowed on lots using both the split and the two-unit provisions — verify against current §66411.7. Yield often trails stacked ADUs once standards are applied.

### State Density Bonus Law
*Updated 2026-10 (corrected).*
- **Unlocks:** Density above base (sliding 20–50%; stackable up to a further 50% under AB 1287; 80% for fully affordable, no density cap near a major transit stop), plus concessions/incentives (1–5), waivers of standards, and statewide parking caps, in exchange for affordable units. Projects of 5+ units.
- **Citation:** Gov. Code §65915 (and §§65914.1, 65915.2, 65915.5); expanded stacking under AB 1287 (2023).
- **Recent changes:**
  - *In effect 1 Jan 2026 (2025 bills):* SB 92 — concessions cannot raise commercial floor area beyond 2.5× the base zone's commercial FAR; SB 92 / AB 87 — no concessions or waivers required for hotel/transient lodging in a mixed-use project.
  - *AG opinion, April 2025:* local inclusionary requirements cannot be applied to the bonus units.
  - *Effective 1 Jan 2027 (2026 bills):* AB 2433 — homeownership incentives, law applies even if the applicant doesn't cite it, bonus includes floor area where FAR sets base density, benefits apply anywhere within the development (details from a law-firm summary — verify against chaptered text). AB 939 (for-sale affordability) and AB 2480 (student housing) — known only from the Governor's one-line descriptions; verify.
  - *SB 79 projects:* can stack the density bonus on SB 79 standards, with up to three additional concessions at higher densities.
- **Disqualifiers / notes:** Requires an affordability set-aside and covenants (rentals commonly 55 years). **Density bonus alone does not impose prevailing wage**; pairing it with SB 35/SB 423, AB 2011, SB 6, or SB 79 (over 85 ft) does, through those statutes' labor standards. Process stays discretionary (CEQA, hearings) unless paired with a streamlining law. Compare against ADU yield before recommending — density bonus carries more strings. SB 1383 (2026, concessions tied to local labor standards above 85 ft) was reported signed by one firm but is not on the Governor's list — verify before citing.

### SB 330 — Housing Crisis Act
*Updated 2026-10.*
- **Unlocks:** Preliminary application vesting (freezes standards/fees), a five-hearing limit, limits on downzoning and added standards, no-net-loss / replacement obligations on protected units.
- **Citation:** Gov. Code §§65913.10, 65940, 66300, 66300.5, 66300.6 (HCA); preliminary application at §65941.1; five-hearing rule at §65905.5. **Now permanent:** AB 130 (2025) removed the sunset.
- **Key mechanics:** Preliminary application (17 items + fee) → full application within 180 days; supply missing items within 90 days of an incompleteness finding; vesting lost if units or square footage grow ≥20% (excluding density bonus). Once complete, a project meeting objective GP/zoning standards faces no more than five hearings (continuances, workshops, appeals count; legislative approvals such as rezonings do not). *Effective 1 Jan 2027:* SB 1014 (2026) — nonbinding good-faith estimate of on-/off-site improvements within 30 business days of a complete preliminary or project application.
- **Disqualifiers / notes:** A protective lever more than a yield lever. Replacement obligations (deed-restricted, rent-controlled, lower-income-occupied in last 5 years, Ellis withdrawals in last 10) can cut the other way on redevelopment of existing rental — screen for protected units early.

### SB 35 / SB 423 — ministerial streamlining (SMAP)
*Updated 2026-10.*
- **Unlocks:** Ministerial, CEQA-exempt approval for qualifying multifamily housing (2+ attached units) in jurisdictions HCD has found behind on RHNA, on a fixed timeline.
- **Citation:** Gov. Code §65913.4 (SB 35, Ch. 366/2017; extended and broadened by SB 423, Ch. 778/2023). **Sunset 1 Jan 2036.**
- **Key mechanics:** Applicability by HCD statutory determination (10% or 50% lower-income tier, or not subject) — check HCD's current determination for the jurisdiction. Projects ≤10 units exempt from the set-aside. Timelines: consistency review 60 days (≤150 units) / 90 days (>150); objective design review 90 / 180 days. Tribal consultation after notice of intent can end eligibility. *Effective 1 Jan 2027:* AB 2390 (Ch. 799/2026) clarifies review of modifications to approved projects. HCD released draft updated guidelines for comment in 2026 (comments closed 20 Jul 2026); whether final guidelines are adopted — verify.
- **Labor tiers:** >10 units = prevailing wage; 50+ units = also apprenticeship and health care expenditures; buildings over 85 ft = skilled and trained workforce (exceptions for fully affordable). ≤10 units exempt.
- **Disqualifiers / notes:** Infill site in an urbanized area (≥75% perimeter urban); residential/mixed-use zoning (≥2/3 residential). Site exclusions: wetlands, prime/statewide farmland, mapped habitat, conservation easement/habitat plan land, hazardous waste site, floodway/certain flood zones, fault zone (unless seismic code), VHFHSZ without mitigation; demolition of rent-restricted/controlled or tenant-occupied (last 10 years) housing or a historic structure. SB 423 opened parts of the coastal zone. SB 6 and SB 79 projects can use this process when otherwise eligible.

### AB 2011 — Affordable Housing and High Road Jobs Act
*Updated 2026-10.*
- **Unlocks:** Ministerial, CEQA-exempt residential on sites where office, retail, or parking is a principally permitted use. Two paths: 100% lower-income, or mixed-income on commercial corridors.
- **Citation:** Gov. Code §§65912.100–65912.140. Effective 1 Jul 2023; **sunset 1 Jan 2033.**
- **Recent changes:** AB 2243 (2024) added regional mall sites and some sites near freeways. AB 893 (2025) added campus development zones near public colleges and limited the site to the area disturbed by construction. *Effective 1 Jan 2027:* AB 2118 (Ch. 579/2026) bars AB 2011 standards from limiting mixed use in qualifying projects.
- **Disqualifiers / notes:** Corridor width, distance from industrial uses and freeways, environmental exclusions, limits on vacant sites, replacement of existing housing. Prevailing wage on all projects; apprenticeship and health care at 50+ units. Powerful where base zoning is commercial and the prospect wants housing.

### SB 6 — Middle Class Housing Act (housing on commercial land) — NEW 2026-10
- **Unlocks:** Housing as an allowed use on sites where office, retail, or parking is principally permitted, without a rezoning.
- **Citation:** Gov. Code §65852.24 (SB 6, 2022).
- **Disqualifiers / notes:** No affordability requirement of its own (local inclusionary applies). Requires prevailing wage and a skilled and trained workforce. No ministerial process of its own — follows the normal local process (HAA protection and density bonus available) unless the project also qualifies for SB 35/SB 423 streamlining.

### SB 79 — transit-oriented housing (rail and BRT) — NEW 2026-10
- **Unlocks:** Housing of 5+ units as an allowed use on residential, mixed-use, and commercial sites within ½ mile of a qualifying rail or BRT stop, with state minimum height, density, and residential FAR standards that rise closer to the stop. **Operative 1 Jul 2026.**
- **Citation:** Gov. Code §§65912.155–65912.162 (SB 79, 2025).
- **Disqualifiers / notes:** Urban transit counties only (more than 15 passenger rail stations) — reportedly eight counties incl. Sacramento, Los Angeles, San Diego, Orange, and four Bay Area counties; HCD publishes the controlling list and MPOs map the stops — verify the county and stop tier (e.g., SacRT light rail reportedly Tier 2). Unincorporated areas generally deferred to the 7th housing element cycle. Standards tiered by service type (heavy rail / very high-frequency commuter rail above light rail / BRT) and distance (adjacent, ¼ mile, ¼–½ mile in larger cities). Strings: average unit ≤1,750 net habitable sq ft; minimum 30 du/ac; affordable set-aside at 11+ units; labor standards above 85 ft; anti-displacement limits. Cities may adopt an HCD-reviewed alternative plan or exempt/defer certain sites. Does not itself change CEQA, but eligible projects can use SB 35/SB 423 and the density bonus (up to three extra concessions). SB 722 (2026) excludes mobilehome and RV park sites.

### AB 507 — adaptive reuse — NEW 2026-10
- **Unlocks:** By-right conversion of qualifying existing nonresidential buildings to housing in most zones other than industrial, with ministerial, CEQA-exempt review. **Operative 1 Jul 2026.**
- **Citation:** AB 507 (Ch. 493/2025) — verify code placement.
- **Disqualifiers / notes:** Affordability and labor requirements apply; limits on impact fees; no parking required for space that has none today. Building and site eligibility criteria — verify against statute.

### AB 2074 — regional transit hub districts — NEW 2026-10
- **Unlocks:** Transit hub districts in cities over 400,000 population; designation due by 1 Jul 2027.
- **Citation:** AB 2074 (2026) — verify chapter number, code placement, and standards.
- **Disqualifiers / notes:** Known only from secondary summaries of the September 2026 signings. Large cities only; nothing to flag until districts are designated. Verify before citing to a client.

### AB 2097 — parking minimum elimination
*Updated 2026-10.*
- **Unlocks:** Removes local minimum parking requirements for most residential, commercial, and other projects within ½ mile of a major transit stop.
- **Citation:** Gov. Code §65863.2. AB 2553 (2024) broadened "major transit stop" to include intersections of two or more bus routes with peak service every **20 minutes** or better (was 15), and stops in the regional transportation plan count.
- **Disqualifiers / notes:** Hotels, event centers, and some housing cases are excepted; for some housing projects the agency can make findings within 30 days that parking is still needed. Removes the requirement, not the option. Frees site area and improves yield/feasibility; pairs with the housing pathways above.

### Housing Accountability Act / Builder's Remedy
*Updated 2026-10.*
- **Unlocks:** Constrains denial or density reduction of housing that meets objective standards (only on a preponderance finding of a specific, unmitigable adverse health/safety impact); stronger protection for affordable and small (≤10 units, <1 ac) projects; Builder's Remedy where the jurisdiction lacks a substantially compliant Housing Element.
- **Citation:** Gov. Code §§65589.5, 65589.5.1, 65589.5.2. AB 1893 (2024) is the governing Builder's Remedy rewrite (affordability mix, allowable density, objective standards that still apply; sites next to heavy industrial excluded).
- **Recent changes:** AB 1633 / AB 1413 — failure to make a required CEQA decision (and missed post-entitlement permit deadlines) can count as a disapproval. AB 712 (2025; since 2026) — fee awards to prevailing developers in many housing cases. SB 838 (2025) — hotel exclusion (verify scope).
- **Key mechanics:** Agency must identify inconsistencies in writing within 30 days of completeness (≤150 units) or 60 days (>150), or the project is deemed consistent. Density bonus increases, concessions, and waivers count as consistent.
- **Disqualifiers / notes:** Builder's Remedy is fact-sensitive and politically contested; turns on the Housing Element's status on the day the application is complete. Flag as a possibility for counsel/ISR analysis, not a casual recommendation. Whether a Housing Element counts as compliant only on an HCD or court finding (reported as AB 1886, 2024) — verify.

### SB 684 / SB 1123 — Starter Home Revitalization Act (small-lot subdivision)
*Updated 2026-10.*
- **Unlocks:** Ministerial subdivision map and approval for up to 10 parcels / 10 homes (ADUs/JADUs don't count toward the 10 since SB 1123); 60 days to act on a complete application or **deemed approved**; no CEQA. Fee simple, CID, TIC, or CLT ownership.
- **Citation:** Gov. Code §§65852.28, 65913.4.5, 66499.41 (SB 684, 2023; SB 1123, 2024, operative **1 Jul 2025**). SB 684's original effective date reported as 1 Jan or 1 Jul 2024 — verify if it matters.
- **Limits:** Multifamily-zoned lot ≤5 ac, or (since 1 Jul 2025) vacant single-family-zoned lot ≤1.5 ac; urban site in an incorporated city or urbanized parts of larger counties; new parcels ≥600 sq ft (1,200 sq ft on single-family land); average unit ≤1,750 net habitable sq ft; minimum density tied to zoning and Housing Element inventory (exact rule, incl. a reported 66%-of-max-density test — verify). Agency may not apply standards that physically preclude the allowed density; no CUP (HCD letters to Oakland and Hayward, 2025).
- **Coming change — SB 1116 (Ch. 808/2026), applications received on or after 1 Jan 2027:** replaces the "substantially surrounded by urban uses" test; height measured as physical height, not stories; limits front/internal setbacks; parcels as small as 480 / 960 sq ft in some multifamily cases; stairs and enclosed bike parking excluded from the 1,750 sq ft average; vacant = no permanent structure (unless abandoned/untenantable); blocking private covenants unenforceable outside CIDs; liberal construction.
- **Disqualifiers / notes:** SB 35 site-exclusion list applies by cross-reference (farmland, wetlands, habitat, fire); no displacement of rent-controlled or recently tenant-occupied housing; local inclusionary applies. Local implementation still settling — confirm live text.

### AB 1751 — townhome ministerial path — NEW 2026-10
- **Unlocks:** A separate ministerial path for townhome projects, with its own affordability and density rules. **Effective 1 Jan 2027.**
- **Citation:** AB 1751 (2026) — verify chapter number, code placement, and eligibility criteria.
- **Disqualifiers / notes:** Compare against SB 684 / SB 1123 (and SB 1116) for small-lot ownership product.

### CEQA infill-housing exemption and near-miss path — NEW 2026-10
- **Unlocks:** Statutory CEQA exemption for qualifying housing and mostly-residential mixed-use projects — the key lever for **discretionary** infill that no ministerial pathway reaches. Signed 30 Jun 2025.
- **Citation:** Pub. Res. Code §21080.66 (AB 130, 2025). Near-miss path: SB 131 (2025) — section number unconfirmed (one summary cites PRC §21080.1, which appears wrong) — verify.
- **Criteria:** Urbanized area; site ≤20 ac, previously developed or surrounded by urban uses; minimum density; consistent with General Plan and zoning; sensitive sites excluded; tribal consultation; Phase I ESA; labor standards for some projects. Agency must act within 30 days after tribal consultation and consistency review end.
- **Near-miss:** A housing project that misses an exemption on a single condition limits CEQA review to that one issue. SB 131 also exempted rezonings implementing a certified Housing Element (with exclusions).
- **Disqualifiers / notes:** Builder's-remedy project acreage limit under SB 158 — sources conflict (cap cut from 5 to 4 ac for the exemption vs. bar on BR projects over 4 ac using the near-miss path) — verify. Class 32 categorical infill exemption (CEQA Guidelines §15332, ≤5 ac) remains the fallback. Updated CEQA Guidelines reportedly due 1 Jan 2027 — verify.

### Williamson Act (constraint lever)
*Updated 2026-10 (corrected).*
- **Unlocks / constrains:** Agricultural land under contract carries tax benefits but restricts development; nonrenewal or cancellation is required before conversion.
- **Citation:** Gov. Code §51200 et seq. (nonrenewal §§51245–51246; cancellation §§51280–51287, fee §51283; FSZ §51296).
- **Disqualifiers / notes:** A constraint to surface, not an unlock. **Nonrenewal runs nine to ten years** after notice depending on the contract (DOC FAQ: 9-year minimum term; statute commonly cited as not less than 10 years at §51244 — verify preferred wording), no fee, assessment steps up toward full value. Cancellation requires a public hearing and findings (verify §51282 finding wording) and a **fee of 12.5%** of unrestricted market value (**25% for Farmland Security Zone** contracts); usually goes through CEQA with the project. Rescission into an ag conservation easement or (where the county participates) a solar use easement, and eminent domain, are other exits. Check DOC's Enrollment Finder and recorded nonrenewal notices — an old notice may mean expiry is near. Flag if the parcel is in an ag preserve.

### Ellis Act (rental withdrawal)
- **Unlocks / constrains:** Permits withdrawal of rental units from the market for redevelopment, subject to local replacement/relocation rules.
- **Citation:** Gov. Code §7060–7060.7.
- **Disqualifiers / notes:** Interacts heavily with local ordinances (e.g., LA replacement-unit determinations). Surface where redevelopment displaces existing rental. A withdrawal in the last 15 years excludes SB 9; withdrawn units are protected units under the HCA (last 10 years).

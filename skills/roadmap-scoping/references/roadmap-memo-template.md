# Strategy Session write-up — memo template

The client-facing write-up for a Strategy Session (planner level 2), delivered with the session. The format was proven on the Entitlement Roadmap memos for Lemon Hill (Two 24 LLC, Sept 15, 2026, 7 pages) and Riego Rd (Sundance Development Corp, Sept 21, 2026), sold under the retired Roadmap rungs; the structure carries over. The draft Roadmap in the Cox Entitlement Model is the internal record behind it; its deficiencies feed sections 6 and 8. Keep the section order; drop a section only when it has nothing to say for this site, and say so in one line rather than leaving a gap. Memo format with the Cox cover and footer (`cox-document-formatting`); Navy #1B2A6B, Gold #F5A623, Arial.

**Standing header, verbatim, under the MEMORANDUM block:**
> For planning purposes. Not a scope of work, a fee proposal, or a commitment to perform work. Costs and durations are planning estimates. Agency review timelines are outside our control.

To / From / Date / Re: client and entity; Chris Cox, Principal Planner; the date; "Strategy Session: Entitlement Roadmap for <address>; APN <apns>; approximately <acres> acres".

## 1.0 The Project
Two or three sentences: what they own or are buying, where, how it is zoned and designated, and what this roadmap covers ("the best use for the site, how it gets approved, and what it takes to get there").

## 2.0 The Bottom Line
Three labeled lines a client can repeat to a partner:
- **WHAT TO BUILD** — product, form, scale ("market rate garden apartments, two to three stories, surface parking, roughly 175 to 210 units").
- **HOW IT GETS APPROVED** — the approval set in one sentence ("rezone to R-3, take a state density bonus, complete design review, clear CEQA with an infill exemption; rental, so no subdivision map").
- **WHAT IT TAKES** — one cost figure and one duration, with the assumption that carries them ("about $160,000 and 16 to 24 months to entitlement, assuming the site is clear of sensitive resources").
One note up front if there is a single issue the client raised that the recommendation turns on.

## 3.0 The Property
Table 1, Property record: location and APNs; size; zoning (now and proposed); General Plan designation and plan status (adopted, date); parking rule in force; transit; nearby water; habitat plan coverage (inside or outside, by map). Every row has a source behind it in the screen; a row without one reads "[FACT NEEDED]".

## 4.0 Historic Aerial Analysis
What the multi-year aerial record shows: condition, disturbance, structures, ponding or its absence in wet-season frames, trees to check. Figure 1: annotated current aerial plus a filmstrip of dated frames; Google attribution retained on the image. Close with what field verification will confirm.

## 5.0 What to Build
The market read in plain terms: rents or comps, what form pencils and why, realistic yield versus the paper maximum, what buyers of entitled sites want. State what Cox is not: "this is a desktop read, not an appraisal or a market study."

## 6.0 How It Gets Approved
The discretionary approvals as a bulleted list, each with its approval body and what it does for the project (rezone; density bonus and the covenant it carries; site plan and design review; CEQA pathway and the fallback if a feature turns up; subdivision map or not; any resource permits and the structural dependency they bring). Then what the client holds when these are approved, and what comes later (building permits, construction method).

## 7.0 The Entitlement Design
The plan set the agency reviews and the disciplines that produce it (architecture to design development; civil preliminary grading, drainage, utilities, stormwater, access; landscape and open space). Say that it becomes the basis for construction drawings so nothing is redrawn.

## 8.0 What Could Change It
The two to four things the paid next step checks, each with the cost it carries if it goes the other way: biology and wetlands screening and its assumption; the agency's early review that converts the estimate into a firm number; the survey; anything site-specific (Williamson Act, easements, a creek). This section is the scope of the proposal that follows.

## 9.0 Schedule and Budget
Figure 2, the graphic schedule: each step as a bar with its window and budget on the bar and the entitlement milestone marked. Under it, the total to entitlement, the assumption that carries it, and the sentence that the figures are planning estimates set in each Task Order. (Complex tier: add the program budget table from the Riego format — Item / Low / High / Confidence / Basis and what drives it / When the cost lands — in four blocks: Cox services, consultants retained by the client, agency fees, and a "not yet quantifiable" block that names drivers and reference unit costs instead of a bracket.)

## 10.0 References
Cited sources only: code sections, the General Plan and its adoption date, Master EIR SCH number, fee schedules with effective dates, the habitat plan boundary, NWI/CARI. Nothing from memory.

## 11.0 Terms
> We meet professional standards for planning, environmental, civil engineering, architecture, and landscape architecture work in California. We do not guarantee approvals or agency timelines. The figures here are planning estimates, not a fee proposal. Fees are set in each Task Order. Prepared for <entity> for this property and this project.

## 12.0 Next Steps
Name the proposal that follows: usually Task Order 1 at Q2 Underwrite (2A Field Verification, then 2B Site Plan and Alternatives, with engineering front and center), built from the draft Roadmap's deficiencies, so the client sees the project take shape before permitting (Q4) is discussed; then, what it resolves from section 8, and that Cox is ready to start. If the client bought a Site Walk, say its fee is credited toward 2A Field Verification if signed within 90 days of the walk. Signature block: Chris Cox, Principal Planner / Landscape Architect.

---

### Complex-tier additions (the scale test came out complex)
The program budget table in 9.0; a one-page board summary on the cover side (bottom line, the schedule figure, the budget total with its confidence composition) when the client needs a board, investment committee, lender or agency package; and a specialist desk read (biology, engineering, cultural) as a short attachment under its author's name where a mapped resource triggered the tier.

### Planner Review
Not this memo. A Planner Review is the client's Screen and Scenarios read, checked, corrected and signed in the portal: the approvals in order, cost and schedule ranges with their assumptions, and the questions to answer next. Its next step is a Strategy Session (the Planner Review fee comes off it, up to $500) or Task Order 1.
